# iML1515 broad organic-source pool: literature and simulator design review

**Final pool size: 48 exchanges (16 mandatory + 32 discretionary). Recommended maximum K: 10.** This is a curated pool of soluble carbon sources and directly depleted complex-medium organic nutrients for unmodified *E. coli* K-12 MG1655. K is a chosen coverage cap within the allowed K ≤20 design space; neither pool membership nor the literature proves joint uptake under oxygen limitation.

The [CSV](../../data/reference/iML1515_broad_organic_source_pool.csv) contains exact candidate/model names. The [source record](../../data/reference/iML1515_broad_pool_sources.md) contains **56 primary-paper records**, evidence locators, access limits, full citations and author-year BibTeX. All 185 candidates have individual decisions below.

## Scope, provenance and evidence rules

Search cutoff: **2026-10-05**. The user specified **unmodified K-12 MG1655**. The current broad G component of the D/E generators samples positive oxygen-uptake magnitudes of **1–10 mmol gDW⁻¹ h⁻¹**; these are flux caps, not dissolved-oxygen concentrations, and do not reproduce the aeration of a literature assay. A 30–37°C ordinary laboratory baseline is assumed where temperature changes the decision. All claims about native physiology are qualified accordingly.

The authoritative input was `data/reference/iML1515_growth_carbon_sources.csv`: **185 unique candidate exchanges**. Its positive `biomass_flux` values are model-screen results; the CSV does not establish native expression, transporter regulation, practical solubility, or a measured growth phenotype. No FBA screen or experimental simulation was rerun. Exchange availability and exact names were checked against `models/iML1515.json`; the selected CSV preserves input order. SHA-256 values at audit:

Three claims are kept separate throughout:

1. **P — presence:** the exact compound is identified in a real stream or complex cultivation medium at a stated processing stage. Composition is evidence of supply, not consumption.
2. **U — consumption:** disappearance/uptake of that compound is measured during cultivation. Growth on a whole medium or production of an acid does not demonstrate that every detected constituent was consumed.
3. **E — native physiology:** unmodified MG1655, or clearly identified native K-12 transport/catabolic evidence, supports use under oxygen-compatible conditions. Other strains, recombinant controls, evolved derivatives and temperature/oxygen exceptions are labeled.

For discretionary sources, inclusion requires credible accessible P plus native carbon-use E, or direct native nutrient depletion in a real complex cultivation medium. Direct U in the relevant strain/medium strengthens a carbon-source decision. Mandatory sources are retained independently of this rule and their qualifications remain explicit. Analytical identification with standards is preferred; full composition panels, named enzymatically released products and primary compound-specific Results are used where available. Numeric composition is preferred but is not a universal admission requirement: a resolved relative signal with native consumption in a real complex cultivation medium can establish an organic nutrient. No synthetic sole-substrate recipe alone establishes industrial-stream presence. A free monomer after deliberate processing can qualify; an intact polymer or protein-bound total cannot be substituted without that processing.

Ten L-amino acids and glycine are admitted as **organic nutrients**, because their extracellular depletion during ancestral MG1655 growth is measured in LB [Katz2023]. This is narrower than assuming that every amino acid is a sole-carbon substrate, and broader than a strictly catabolic carbon-source list. The retained positive-model-screen candidates form the available starting universe; other experimentally used amino acids outside that list are reported below. A random one-amino-acid draw still needs a native phenotype check, even though the model screen predicts growth. Nitrogen status and biomass incorporation affect interpretation [Sezonov2007,Maser2020].

Exclusion is either a positive physiological/identity reason or a documented evidence gap. **A gap is not proof of absence or incapacity.** The default pool defers poorly resolved free fractions, trace/bound derivatives, rare intracellular phosphorylated compounds, specific unmeasured peptides, and oil-bound fatty-acid identities. This scope prioritizes usable aqueous industrial/complex nutrients; it does not claim to enumerate every organic molecule MG1655 could ever assimilate. Compounds such as Neu5Ac, glucuronate, glucarate, SQ and fructoselysine are explicitly recognized as physiological or conditional extensions rather than labeled universally unusable.

## Search strategy and coverage

The search began with measured-composition papers for crop/lignocellulosic hydrolysates, cane/beet sugar processing, brewery residues, whey, fruit pomace/must, crude biodiesel glycerol, multiple macroalgae, food-waste fermentation and wastewater-derived acidogenic effluents. It expanded to **cottonseed raffinose-manufacturing molasses, partly saccharified oats, chitin/chitosan processing, yeast extract/LB, phototroph spent medium, and free-versus-bound constituents of dairy/formula and glycated food materials**. Previously fermented streams were considered as potential downstream feeds, with their products kept separate from raw-feed composition.

Query patterns combined a feedstock family with `composition`, `HPLC`, `LC-MS`, `NMR`, `free amino acids`, `substrate consumption`, `Escherichia coli`, and cultivation terms. Candidate-focused queries combined compound names/aliases with `MG1655`, `K-12`, `wild type`, `growth`, `uptake`, `aerobic`, `anaerobic`, `adaptation`, or `engineering`. Targeted follow-ups addressed citrate/ferric citrate, ethanol, butyrate, cellobiose, propanediol, ethanolamine, putrescine/GABA/choline/allantoin, ascorbate, glycolate, higher maltodextrins, stereoisomeric sugars/acids, nucleosides/nucleotides, phosphate derivatives, glutathione/cysteine, fatty acids, sialic acids, glucarate, sulfoquinovose and glycation derivatives. Every candidate was mapped against the retrieved composition/physiology evidence and an explicit individual decision; weakly supported classes were not promoted from untargeted annotation inventories.

Original tables and supplementary material were retrieved where accessible. Publisher, PubMed or the primary paper verified every cited reference; DOI registry metadata supplemented citation fields rather than replacing biological evidence. Access varied: some numerical tables/full texts could not be retrieved, and those limits remain explicit in the source record. This is a structured decision review, not a preregistered PRISMA review; no invented database hit counts or claim of exhaustive publication coverage is made.

A further targeted pass across yeast autolysates and fermented seafood found resolved extracellular nucleosides [Zhao2005,Koizumi1996]. Paired with the WT MG1655 carbon assays [Shin2026], this supports cytidine, adenosine, uridine and inosine. Guanosine remains conditional: identified availability and MG1655 nitrogen-source agar growth do not supply the default's carbon-growth or actual complex-feed depletion evidence [Mackie2014,Rodionova2021]. Additional beet-pulp, spruce, bagasse/wheat/corn/willow and uptake-regulatory papers widened the family comparison and qualified K without adding further identities [Berlowska2017,Kumar2026,Rumbold2009]. Targeted searches for uronates, phosphorylated nutrients, free/bound amino acids, lipids and cryptic substrates did not close the remaining paired evidence gaps. No fixed hit count or formal saturation claim is made: accessible evidence has reached practical decision stability, with unresolved free fractions and strain/oxygen phenotypes the main gaps.

### Material qualifications of the selected pool

The amino-acid decision uses the **ancestral, unevolved MG1655** trajectories in Katz2023's named S2 worksheets. All eleven selected nutrients decline by 24 h; this does not quantify how much carbon was oxidized rather than incorporated into biomass. Separate WT carbon assays support proline, aspartate, asparagine and serine [Shin2026]; sole-carbon evidence remains incomplete for the other selected nutrients. Hydrolysate presence alone is not the uptake proof. LB can also provide peptide-bound nutrients [Sezonov2007], and different yeast-extract lots differ [Tachibana2021].

Katz2023's S1 Table has both a separately named **Ribose** worksheet and an unresolved **Xyl_Rib_Ara** feature. The separately identified ribose signal supports ribose presence; the unresolved feature is not assigned to three independent feeds. Likewise **Manni_Sorb** cannot establish separate mannitol/sorbitol concentrations. Ribose accumulates late in long-term culture, so late accumulation is not described as consumption. Direct native ribose physiology is established by Angles2017.

The sugar-assimilation controls in Abe2017 carry empty vectors but preserve native catabolic genes. They provide native-pathway capacity evidence, not literal plasmid-free performance in waste hydrolysate. Cellobiose is scored only **slight growth**, while the selected higher maltodextrins have **clear growth**. This distinction motivates the conservative cellobiose deferral. Glucose represses maltose utilization in the same study.

Two lactate exchanges represent two demonstrated native capacities [Brutinel2012]. Most feedstock lactate assays are not enantiomer specific. Both capacities are retained for breadth, but an unresolved lactate measurement counts once for stream richness, and must not supply two full independent uptake budgets. D-lactate produced after crude-glycerol fermentation [Chen2014] is an example of an explicitly identified downstream feed rather than an initial crude-glycerol component. L-malate mapping is biologically plausible and supported by native use, but feed-specific L/D proportions need verification.

Fumarate inclusion uses resolved apple-pomace presence [Martau2021] plus native K-12 transport/growth physiology [Davies1999]; it does not claim that MG1655 was grown on that pomace. Propionate supports slow aerobic growth [Simonte2017], whereas the tested anaerobic acceptors did not establish its degradation. Glycolate has direct native MG1655 growth in the untreated control [Yang2019], with strain-dependent lag qualifications [Hohmann2024]. Oxygen flux caps cannot be converted into these studies' growth rates without a separate calibration.

The four selected nucleosides use resolved post-processing presence plus **actual WT MG1655 carbon-source growth**, not purine-nitrogen salvage as a surrogate. Yeast autolysate is a deliberately processed biomass ingredient, and fish sauce is a fermented food stream; neither paper proves MG1655 growth in the undiluted matrix. Zhao2005's nucleoside concentrations are described but not individually tabulated, so they support identity/availability without a 1 mM richness count. Low oxygen and catabolite regulation can still suppress their use.

## Counting distinct available substrates in individual streams

For a quantitative count, a compound is **present at threshold t** when the paper reports a resolved identity and concentration **c ≥ t**, using only the same prepared stream/sample or explicitly identified treatment panel. Where the paper provides an LOQ, c must also exceed that LOQ. Otherwise the published quantified concentration is accepted with the method limitation stated; no universal LOD is invented. Unknown peaks, summed "other" components, below-LOQ estimates, protein-bound totals without release, and zero/non-detected values do not become additional available substrates.

The main design threshold is **1 mM**, chosen as a practical minor-to-bulk nutrient inventory cutoff. It is a design convention, not a universal uptake or growth-contribution threshold. The 0.1 mM count gives more permissive minor/trace coverage; 10 mM emphasizes abundant components. Molar rather than mass thresholds permit comparison across molecules; `mM = 1000 × (g/L) / molecular_mass(g/mol)`. Amino-acid μM values are divided by 1000. The sensitivity analysis also uses 0.01, 0.25, 0.5, 1 and 10 mM. These calculations describe feed inventory, not uptake flux: concentration cannot directly set mmol gDW⁻¹ h⁻¹ without biomass/time/kinetic assumptions.

- **23 identities** result if the ten reported selected amino-acid values also represent available organic nutrients after the specified preparation/release assumptions.

## Recommendation for K and the one-million-sample budget

**Recommend K = 10 for both D and E.** K is the maximum number of distinct selected exchanges offered in a generated medium before feasibility filtering; active uptake can be lower. This is a **chosen coverage cap** informed by practical cross-family composition panels and finite sampling, not a literature-established maximum or supported percentile. The evidence does not uniquely identify 10.

At the adopted 1 mM convention, a cap of 10 accommodates the reconstructed 2–9 eligible identities in the quantified family panels, including the 8-monomer beet hydrolysates and 7-identity spruce/algal examples. This does not prove that their complete inventories contain ≤10 available nutrients. K = 4–6 excludes some measured algal and beet panels; K = 8 excludes the qualified 9-identity corn count and gives little headroom for unassayed nutrients. K = 10 gives modest room while restraining very sparse high-cardinality sampling. K = 12–20 broadens possible mixtures without a demonstrated cross-family need in these panels and expands the space sharply. Rich extracts, combined feeds, and permissive 0.1 mM corn compositions with 11–23 eligible identities are intentionally truncated by the default; their realism is acknowledged. Retaining all 16 mandatory sources in the pool does not require K ≥16.

For N = 48, the unordered identity-subset count is S(K) = sum(C(48,k), k=1…K). It excludes oxygen, uptake caps, salts and biological state. B/S is an average diagnostic **only** for uniform sampling over all subsets; it is not the proposed sampler or a requirement to exhaust the space.

### Explicit assumed sampling distribution

For the budget argument, use **P(k) = (2/3)^(k−1) / Z**, k = 1,…,10, with Z = sum((2/3)^(j−1), j=1,…,10). Conditional on k, draw source identities uniformly without replacement from the 48-member pool. This truncated geometric distribution emphasizes sparse mixtures and retains a small exploratory tail. It is a proposed design assumption, not an assertion that a generator was changed or that this is an empirical feedstock-frequency distribution.

Each source appears in approximately **58,823** rows; each specified pair is jointly offered in approximately **4,146** rows and each specified triple in **372**. Uniform cardinality P(k)=1/10 would allocate 100,000 rows to each k, yielding about 2,083 per singleton, 88.7 per exact pair and 5.78 per exact triple. Uniform sampling over all S(10) subsets instead places about 75% of draws at k=10 and scarcely samples singles. These are materially different designs; S(K) alone does not specify the distribution. The sparse-mixture assumption is preferred for broad coverage of individual source effects and low-order interactions. Family-conditioned samples would preserve measured co-occurrence better, but need a separate design/implementation.

### Continuous bounds and acceptance

Even one identity subset has a continuum of conditions. As a diagnostic, three uptake levels per offered source produce 3^k cap vectors per k-subset, before varying oxygen. A k-source medium with independent uptake and oxygen caps has at least k+1 continuous coordinates; carbon-normalizing a total budget would instead couple those coordinates. Consequently one million rows cannot densely cover every identity/bound combination, and many repeated small subsets remain useful at differing bounds.

The current G-style values (organic uptake caps approximately 0.05–5 and oxygen caps 1–10 mmol gDW⁻¹ h⁻¹) are flux constraints, not the literature's millimolar composition. The concentration counts motivate cardinality only. Independent caps can overemphasize minor nutrients, erase feed-family ratios, or double-budget achiral lactate. No direct concentration-to-flux conversion is justified here. Accepted datasets should eventually report realized cardinality, source/pair frequencies and feasibility rejection; the expectations above apply to proposed draws before those effects. This review makes no code or experiment change.

The entire 1,000,000 is assumed available to broad draws for the arithmetic. If D reserves rows for A/B distributions, apply the remaining broad budget instead: an 800,000-row broad allocation reduces all expectations by 20%, while S(K) is unchanged. This further favors small mixtures and makes the larger K options harder to cover. K=10 is a practical truncation, not a promise of comprehensive coverage or improved downstream accuracy.

## Remaining biological limitations and verification

- **Joint uptake and oxygen:** aerobic single-source capacity does not establish co-utilization under 1–10 oxygen-uptake caps. Catabolite repression, induction, diauxie and respiratory limitation remain material [Angles2017,Abe2017,Davies1999,Simonte2017].
- **Organic nutrients:** eleven amino-acid/glycine admissions reflect direct native depletion in LB, not established sole-carbon phenotypes for every draw. Nitrogen and biomass salvage affect uptake [Katz2023,Sezonov2007,Maser2020].
- **Identity and accessibility:** amino-acid free fractions, stereochemistry, autolysis, polymer processing, oxidation state, fatty-acid phase and inhibitors are not captured by membership alone [Schwalbach2012,Pan2011,Li2021,Zhao2005].
- **Strain/model mismatch:** empty-vector controls support native pathways but are not plasmid-free stream assays. Adaptation, cryptic pathways and MG1655 stock differences qualify phenotype transfers [Abe2017,VanHofwegen2016,Neelakanta2009,Lee2010,Soupene2003,Mackie2014].
- **Coverage:** the sample budget supports source effects and low-order combinations far better than exact large subsets or their continuous caps. The cited panels do not establish a representative upper bound, complete organic inventory, percentile or downstream model accuracy.
- **Access:** some primary numerical panels/supplements are inaccessible or incomplete. Metadata verification is explicitly distinguished from full biological-data inspection; deferred evidence gaps can be revisited.

Verified: **185 candidates = 48 included + 137 excluded**, exact candidate/model exchange names, candidate order, uniqueness, all 15 AMN inputs plus MINN glucose, threshold arithmetic, subset counts, citation keys/BibTeX and local links. The candidate/model hashes are unchanged. Only the three authorized output files were revised; no code, generator, existing training/test dataset, maintained experiment note or Research Base was edited. No new cultivation or generator/model run validates the recommendations.
